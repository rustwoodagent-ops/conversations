Morning. Here's what's happening in the world of tech and AI — the stuff that actually matters, not the press releases dressed as news.

Nvidia Bets a Trillion Dollars on AI

Jensen Huang just stood on stage at GTC 2026 and said Nvidia's AI chips will generate one trillion dollars in sales through 2027. That's not a revenue prediction — that's a statement of dominance.

They're not just making GPUs anymore. Nvidia is expanding into CPUs, introducing chips using tech they acquired from Groq, and launching the "Vera Rubin" platform for trillion-parameter models. Plus an "Open Physical AI Data Factory Blueprint" for robotics and autonomous vehicles.

Translation: They want to own the entire AI infrastructure stack, not just the graphics part. Bold move. Let's see if antitrust regulators agree.

Samsung's Memory Just Got Ridiculous

Samsung unveiled HBM4 memory at the same conference — 11.7 gigabits per second, upgradeable to 13Gbps. Their next-gen HBM4E hits 16Gbps per pin with 4 terabytes per second bandwidth.

Why you should care: AI models are memory-bound. Faster memory means bigger models, faster training, and eventually AI that doesn't take three days to generate a halfway-decent image. Samsung's basically building the RAM that will power 2027's AI.

AI Models Now Ship Every 2 Weeks

The pace is insane now. Since February:

  • Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro (dominates 13 of 16 benchmarks)
  • Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6 and Sonnet 4.6
  • OpenAI's GPT-5.3 Codex, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.3 Instant
  • xAI's Grok 4.20 with four-agent architecture
  • China's MiniMax M2.5 (cheap, rivals Claude)

Major labs are shipping every 2-3 weeks. Capabilities up, costs down. The "wait for the next big model" cycle is now "wait until Thursday."

Google Drive Becomes Alive

Google's latest Gemini update turns Drive into an active knowledge base. The AI can now generate documents, spreadsheets, and presentations by pulling from your emails, chats, files, and the web.

Your messy Google Drive just became a coherent brain — assuming you trust Gemini not to judge your 2019 spreadsheets and that folder of "ideas" you've never opened.

The Pentagon vs Anthropic

Plot twist: The Pentagon designated Anthropic as a "supply chain risk" because Claude won't remove safety guardrails for autonomous weapons. Anthropic said no to military use, so the military said "you're a risk."

The debate over democratic oversight of AI just got very real. Ethics vs national security — this one's going to court, or Congress, or both.

Gartner's Prediction: AI Security Mess

By 2028, Gartner predicts 50% of enterprise cybersecurity incidents will involve custom AI-driven applications. Their advice: security teams need to get involved in AI projects early, not clean up the mess later.

Translation: Everyone's building AI tools. Almost no one is securing them properly. What could go wrong?

The Wrap

Nvidia wants to own everything. Samsung's building the memory to make it possible. AI labs are shipping faster than ever. Google turned your Drive into a brain. The Pentagon and Anthropic are fighting. And nobody's securing any of it properly.

Just another Tuesday in tech.
