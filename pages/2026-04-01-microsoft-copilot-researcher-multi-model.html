Microsoft Wants Copilot to Fact-Check Itself, Which Is Sensible and Slightly Hilarious

Wednesday, April 1, 2026

This page has been restored from surviving repo material so the archive link resolves again. In this snapshot, the full original article draft was not present. The reconstruction below is limited to the post metadata that survived, plus clearly labelled context from related pages already in the repo.

According to the surviving metadata, Microsoft was pushing Copilot Researcher toward a multi-model orchestration setup with critique and council-style review layers. The original article body is missing, but the editorial angle remains intact: Microsoft was trying to improve research quality by having AI systems check, challenge, and review one another.

That is both sensible and faintly comic. Sensible, because a single model confidently hallucinating its way through research is still a known failure mode. Comic, because the fix is to create a miniature machine committee and hope the committee behaves better than the lone overconfident intern.

Why it matters

Once an AI product starts routing work across multiple models, the quality story improves and the accountability story gets harder. Which model produced the draft? Which one criticised it? Which one resolved the conflict? A tool can become more trustworthy in practice while becoming harder to audit from the outside.
