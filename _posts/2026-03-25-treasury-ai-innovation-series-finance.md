# US Treasury Launches AI Innovation Series for Financial Sector

*March 25, 2026*

The US Treasury isn't trying to stop AI in finance—it's trying to help banks do it right. On March 23, 2026, the Treasury and the Financial Stability Oversight Council (FSOC) launched the AI Innovation Series, a public-private initiative designed to accelerate responsible AI adoption across the financial sector.

The message from Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was clear: "A failure to adopt productivity-enhancing technology poses its own risks." The initiative represents a shift from AI skepticism to AI enablement—regulators recognizing that standing still is riskier than moving forward, provided the movement is thoughtful.

## Four Roundtables, One Goal

The AI Innovation Series will feature four roundtables bringing together financial institutions, technology firms, regulators, and domain experts. The objective isn't to write new rules—it's to understand what AI can do for finance and how to scale it safely.

Topics will include high-value AI use cases, practical strategies for scaling innovation, and approaches for maintaining safety and soundness. The format recognizes that regulators don't have all the answers; the best path forward emerges from dialogue between the institutions building AI and the regulators overseeing it.

## The AI Lexicon: Speaking the Same Language

One of the quietest but most significant releases from the Treasury in March 2026: a shared AI Lexicon for financial services. Developed by the Artificial Intelligence Executive Oversight Group (AIEOG), the lexicon establishes common definitions for key AI concepts.

This matters more than it sounds. Regulatory, technical, legal, and business teams often use the same AI terminology to mean different things. The lexicon aims to eliminate those communication gaps, ensuring that when a bank's compliance team talks about "model risk," they're saying the same thing as the engineering team.

## FS AI RMF: Risk Management for Finance

Alongside the lexicon, Treasury released the Financial Services AI Risk Management Framework (FS AI RMF). Adapted from NIST's AI Risk Management Framework, the FS AI RMF provides practical tools for financial institutions to evaluate AI use cases and manage risks throughout the AI lifecycle.

The framework isn't prescriptive—it doesn't tell banks which AI systems they can and can't use. Instead, it provides a structured approach to identifying, assessing, and mitigating AI risks. Banks can adopt AI more confidently knowing they have a regulatory-compliant risk management structure.

## The Regulatory Balance

Treasury's approach attempts to thread a difficult needle: enabling innovation while maintaining stability. Too much regulation stifles adoption; too little risks systemic failures. The AI Innovation Series represents a bet that collaboration works better than command-and-control.

The initiative aligns with the President's AI Action Plan from July 2025, which emphasized American AI leadership balanced with appropriate safeguards. Treasury is translating that national strategy into sector-specific guidance.

## What Banks Are Actually Doing

Financial institutions are already deploying AI across multiple domains: fraud detection, credit scoring, customer service, algorithmic trading, and regulatory compliance. The Innovation Series aims to help them do it faster and better.

Early use cases focus on areas with clear ROI and manageable risk: automating routine compliance checks, enhancing fraud detection with pattern recognition, and personalizing customer recommendations. More ambitious applications—like AI-driven lending decisions—are proceeding more cautiously.

## The Competitive Dimension

There's a geopolitical angle here. Treasury's initiative is partly about ensuring US financial institutions remain competitive globally. If American banks are paralyzed by regulatory uncertainty while international competitors charge ahead, the US loses financial leadership.

Secretary Bessent's emphasis on "productivity-enhancing technology" signals that Treasury views AI adoption as an economic imperative, not just a technological choice. The financial sector generates billions in economic value; making it more efficient through AI has national economic implications.

The AI Innovation Series won't solve every challenge of AI in finance. But it establishes a template for how regulators and industry can work together: not as adversaries, but as partners in building a financial system that's both innovative and stable.
