# Global Tensions Escalate: Oil Hits $120 as Strait of Hormuz Faces Disruption

**The world's most critical oil chokepoint is under threat.** Following intensive US-Israel strikes on Iranian oil refineries and strategic infrastructure, Iran has retaliated with strikes against Israeli refineries and continued missile and drone attacks targeting US and allied assets. The result: oil futures briefly surged to $120 per barrel, and the Strait of Hormuz—through which one-fifth of global crude oil flows—faced partial closure.

This isn't a regional skirmish. It's a disruption with immediate global economic consequences.

## The Strait of Hormuz: Why It Matters

Approximately 20% of the world's crude oil passes through this narrow waterway connecting the Persian Gulf to the open ocean. When security concerns rendered it largely impassable for a period in early March 2026, oil producers including Iraq and Kuwait were forced to curtail production. The math is simple: no shipping route, no revenue.

## Market Response: Volatility and Uncertainty

Wall Street's VIX index—often called the "fear gauge"—rose by over 30% in a single week. Yet despite the geopolitical turmoil, major US Large Cap stock indices were only down about 2%, remaining within a few percentage points of all-time highs. The market is nervous but not panicking.

Oil prices have since declined to around $100 per barrel after reports that G7 countries are considering releasing strategic oil reserves. But the underlying tension remains, and prices are still significantly elevated compared to pre-conflict levels.

## The Conflict's Scope

US intelligence assessments suggest that while large-scale war is unlikely to quickly topple Iran's regime, the conflict is increasingly penetrating Iran's internal security. Israeli strikes on Iranian checkpoints have reportedly been guided by informants inside the country—a development that signals the conflict's evolution from external strikes to internal pressure.

The reported elevation of Mojtaba Khamenei as successor to his father indicates continuity within the Iranian system rather than disruption. The regime appears stable, which paradoxically means the conflict could continue for an extended period.

## Economic Implications Beyond Oil

Goldman Sachs estimates that a 10% increase in oil prices could raise headline CPI by approximately 0.28 percentage points. With oil up significantly from pre-conflict levels, inflationary pressure is returning at a delicate moment for central banks.

The Federal Reserve, already navigating delayed economic data from the 43-day federal government shutdown spanning late 2025 into early 2026, held interest rates unchanged in its March 17-18 meeting. The FOMC's updated projections show slightly higher inflation expectations for 2026—2.7% compared to December's 2.4%.

## Global Trade Restructuring

Perhaps the most significant long-term impact is how trade data in 2026 is increasingly shaped by geopolitical events rather than cyclical demand. Businesses worldwide are now facing heightened uncertainty regarding shipping routes, oil price volatility, supply chain interruptions, and airspace closures. The era of predictable global commerce is fading.
